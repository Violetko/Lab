Clinical Pilates and chronic low back pain

DMA Clinical Pilates exercises improved clinical and biomechanical outcomes for adults with nonspecific chronic low back pain: a randomized controlled trial

This study examined the effectiveness of DMA Clinical Pilates in improving clinical and biomechanical outcomes against general Pilates and general exercise.

Key findings:

  • Pilates exercises are beneficial for people with nonspecific chronic low back pain.
  • DMA Clinical Pilates exercises, when tailored to an individual's movement preference, are time efficient and easy to learn.
  • This study showed that DMA Clinical Pilates is effective in improving pain, function, disability and movement performance.

Biomechanical effects of exoskeletons

Effectiveness of a passive military exoskeleton in off-loading weight during static and dynamic load carriage: A randomised cross-over study

This study assessed the effectiveness of a passive military exoskeleton in off-loading the weight placed on the body during heavy load carriage under static standing and dynamic walking conditions.

Key findings:

  • This study showed that individuals can reasonably benefit from the UPRISE® Gen 4.0 passive military exoskeleton to lessen the burden of torso-borne load during static quiet standing.
  • The passive military exoskeleton was effective in off-loading some load from the human body during static quiet standing but not dynamic walking on flat and sloped surfaces.
  • The varied response across loads and participants calls for better design and fitting of the military exoskeleton to individual users.

Biomechanics of para-table tennis

Shoulder biomechanics of para-table tennis: a case study of a standing class para-athlete with severe leg impairment

This case study examined the shoulder joint kinematics of a highly skilled para-table tennis player with severe leg impairment.

Key findings:

  • This case study showed that the Class 7 para-player displayed different shoulder joint kinematics when performing table tennis forehand and backhand topspin drives than the able-bodied players.
  • These findings suggest that para-players and coaches should not simply replicate the movement patterns of able-bodied table tennis players.
  • Instead, depending on the nature of the physical impairment, para-players should optimise their own movement strategies for successful performance.

Sport coaching for individuals with disabilities

Measuring upper limb kinematics of forehand and backhand topspin drives with IMU sensors in wheelchair and able-bodied table tennis players

To better understand the biomechanics of para-table tennis players, this study compared the shoulder, elbow, and wrist joint kinematics among able-bodied (AB) and wheelchair players in different classifications.

Key findings:

  • The study revealed that wheelchair table tennis players exhibited distinctly different upper limb kinematics compared with able-bodied participants while executing the forehand and backhand topspin drives.
  • The marked differences in techniques could be due to functional limitations associated with the wheelchair players' physical impairment.
  • Coaches should be aware that traditional table tennis training programs targeting skills and techniques for able-bodied players are unsuitable for para-players.

Treadmill Running Analysis Model (TRAM) for injury management

Predicting running-related injuries from functional, kinetic and kinematic data

This study aimes to develop and compare prediction models of various levels of complexity to predict running-related injuries over 12 months in recreational runners.

Key findings:

  • Running biomechanics were evaluated using clinically friendly tools, including wearable in-shoe force sensors to measure vertical ground reaction forces and 2D video-based kinematic analysis of lower extremities.
  • Prediction models developed using variables obtained from accessible tools are unable to accurately predict future running-related injuries regardless of model complexity.
  • Researchers and practitioners should avoid over-reliance on simple measures for screening injury risks.

Visual-Motor behaviour in field hockey goalkeepers

Development of a virtual reality educational game for field hockey.

The study aims to develop an educational virtual reality game for field hockey goalkeepers train both indoors and outdoors, without requiring another player to be available.

Key findings:

  • Virtual reality is a valuable tool for enhancing field hockey training by offering safe, repeatable, and immersive practice scenarios.
  • The immersive virtual reality app aims to bridge the gap between traditional training limitations and real-world performance demands.
  • We will explore the integration of AI for adaptive training difficulty and personalized feedback.

Kinetic strategy in the rear foot elevated split squat

Effects of external load on joint kinetics in the rear-foot-elevated split squat

This study aims to examine how external load affects kinetics of both the front and the rear legs - the working and the supporting legs, respectively.

Key findings:

  • both the front and rear legs play an important role in performing the .
  • External load likely increases the joint moments of the ankle, knee, and hip for both the front and the rear legs, with the largest effect for the front hip.
  • The large hip and knee joint moments in the rear leg indicates that the rear leg, despite serving as a supporting leg in rear-foot-elevated split squat, may also experience a training stimulus.

Understanding the break shot of 9-ball in billiards through experimental and theoretical approaches

Can a good break shot determine the game outcome in 9-ball?

This study aims to quantify the break shot characteristics and identify their significance in predicting the game outcomes in 9-ball tournaments.

Key findings:

  • In professional 9-ball tournaments, pocketing more balls during the break is associated with more clustered balls remaining on the table.
  • Table clearance and winning of a frame are likely influenced by multiple factors and could not be predicted by the break shot characteristics alone.
  • While Voronoi cell areas could provide an objective measure of the ball distribution on the table, this method did not reveal any association between ball distribution and game outcomes.

Bilateral asymmetry in drop landings

Biomechanics of step-off drop landings are affected by limb dominance and lead limb in task initiation

This study examines the effects of limb dominance and lead limb in task initiation on the kinetics and kinematics of step-off drop landings.

Key findings:

  • Ankle joint quasi-stiffness was significantly greater in the dominant limb than the non-nominant limb.
  • High variability within and between participants in the time difference lead and trail limbs at initial landing contact.
  • Step-off drop landing is not an ideal protocol for examining bilateral asymmetry in lower limb biomechanics due to potential biases introduced by limb dominance and the step-off limb.
